This page explains every term the guide uses, in plain words, with a short example.
Each page in the guide links its terms here.

## Bookkeeping basics

### Organization [#organization]

One set of books in Accly Books, with its own members, numbering, settings and web
address. A group with three companies keeps three organizations.

### Account [#account]

A named bucket that collects one kind of money movement, such as **Cash in Hand**,
**Sales** or **Rent**. Every amount in your books sits in an account.

### Chart of accounts [#chart-of-accounts]

The full list of your accounts, arranged in groups: assets, liabilities, equity,
income and expenses. See [Chart of accounts](/setup/chart-of-accounts).

### Group and leaf account [#group-account]

A **group** holds other accounts, for example **Current Assets**. A **leaf** account
(also called a posting account) holds the amounts, for example **HDFC Bank Current
Account**. You post only to leaf accounts.

### Assets, liabilities and equity [#assets-liabilities-equity]

**Assets** are what the business owns or is owed: cash, bank, stock, machines,
receivables. **Liabilities** are what it owes: payables, loans, taxes due. **Equity** is
the owners' stake: capital plus profits kept in the business.

### Inactive (archived) account [#inactive]

An account, party or payment method you no longer use. It stays in past entries and
reports but no longer appears in pickers for new documents.

### Debit and credit (Dr and Cr) [#debit-credit]

The two sides of every entry. A debit (Dr) increases an asset or an expense; a credit
(Cr) increases a liability, equity or income. Every entry has equal debits and credits.
Example: you receive ₹5,000 cash for a sale. Cash in Hand is debited ₹5,000 and Sales is
credited ₹5,000.

### Journal entry [#journal-entry]

The record of one transaction as debits and credits on two or more accounts. Every
posted document in Accly Books writes its own journal entry for you.

### Voucher [#voucher]

The Indian bookkeeping word for a document that records one transaction, for example a
receipt voucher or a journal voucher. Accly Books calls these documents.

### Adjusting entry [#adjusting-entry]

A journal at the end of a period that corrects or completes the books, such as
depreciation, an accrual or a write-off, before the reports are final.

### Journal [#journal]

A document you use to enter a journal entry by hand, for adjustments that no other
document covers: depreciation, bank charges, a bad-debt write-off, a correction. See
[Journals](/accounting/journals).

### Contra entry [#contra]

An entry that moves money between your own cash and bank accounts, for example
depositing counter cash into the bank. No income or expense is involved.

### Ledger [#ledger]

The list of every entry in one account, in date order, with a running balance. The
**account ledger** shows one account; the **party ledger** shows one customer or
supplier. See [Account ledger](/reports/account-ledger).

### Narration [#narration]

The short description you write on a document or journal, for example "Bank charges
for June 2026 as per statement". It appears in ledgers and the day book.

### Trial balance [#trial-balance]

A list of every account's balance on a date. Total debits must equal total credits.
Accountants check it before preparing the final statements. See
[Trial balance](/reports/trial-balance).

### Profit and loss (P&L) [#profit-and-loss]

The statement of income less expenses for a period. The result is your profit or loss.
See [Profit and loss](/reports/profit-and-loss).

### Balance sheet [#balance-sheet]

The statement of what the business owns (assets), owes (liabilities) and the owners'
stake (equity) on one date. Assets always equal liabilities plus equity. See
[Balance sheet](/reports/balance-sheet).

### Day book [#day-book]

Every entry posted in a period, day by day, across all accounts. A cashier uses it to
check a day's work. See [Day book](/reports/day-book).

### Depreciation [#depreciation]

The part of an asset's cost that you charge as an expense each period because the asset
wears out. Example: a ₹7,40,000 machine depreciated at 10% a year gives ₹18,500 a
quarter.

### Straight-line depreciation [#straight-line]

Depreciation of the same amount each period: cost divided by useful life.

### Accrual [#accrual]

An expense or income recorded in the period it belongs to, even if no money has moved
yet. Quarter-end depreciation is an accrual.

### Write-off and bad debt [#write-off]

A write-off removes an amount you will not collect or pay. A **bad debt** is a customer
balance you write off as a loss. In Accly Books you write off a bad debt with a
[journal](/accounting/journals).

### Closing stock [#closing-stock]

The value of goods unsold at the end of a period. Accly Books does not value stock, so
your accountant books closing stock with a journal if the accounts need it.

### Schedule III [#schedule-iii]

The format the Companies Act requires for a company's balance sheet and P&L. Accly Books
prints management statements; your CA prepares the Schedule III version.

### Overdrawn [#overdrawn]

A bank account with a credit balance: you have spent more than it holds, usually under
an overdraft limit.

### Round-off [#round-off]

The few paise added or removed so a document total is a whole rupee. It posts to a
**Round Off** account.

### Financial year [#financial-year]

India's accounting year, from 1 April to 31 March. "FY 2026-27" runs from 1 Apr 2026 to
31 Mar 2027. Document numbers carry it, for example `INV26-27/7`.

## Parties and balances

### Masters [#masters]

The lists your documents pick from: parties, items, accounts and payment methods.

### Register [#register]

The list of one kind of document, such as the invoice register or the receipts register,
with filters for date, party and status.

### Party [#party]

Anyone you deal with: a customer, a supplier (vendor), a tenant, a donor, an employee or
a government body. One party can hold several roles. See [Parties](/setup/parties).

### Receivable [#receivable]

Money customers owe you. Unpaid invoices sit in **Accounts Receivable**.

### Payable [#payable]

Money you owe suppliers. Unpaid bills sit in **Accounts Payable**.

### Sundry debtors and sundry creditors [#sundry]

Tally's names for receivables (customers who owe you) and payables (suppliers you owe).
Accly Books calls them Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable.

### Control account [#control-account]

An account that holds the total of many party balances, such as Accounts Receivable.
The party ledgers show how that total splits by customer or supplier.

### System account [#system-account]

An account that Accly Books creates and uses for you, such as Accounts Receivable, GST
output accounts and TDS Payable. Documents post to them automatically; you cannot
remove them, and journals refuse some of them.

### Outstanding [#outstanding]

The part of an invoice or bill that is still unpaid. A ₹10,000 invoice with ₹4,000
received has ₹6,000 outstanding.

### Advance [#advance]

Money received from a customer, or paid to a supplier, before the invoice or bill
exists. It waits as a credit until you apply it. See [Receipts](/sales/receipts).

### Credit (unapplied credit) [#credit]

Money in a party's favour that is not yet matched to an invoice or bill: an advance, a
credit note, an opening credit or an overpayment.

### Unapplied amount [#unapplied]

The part of an advance, credit note or overpayment not yet matched to an invoice or
bill.

### Apply credit and allocation [#allocation]

Matching a credit to an invoice or bill, so both close by that amount. Each match is an
**allocation**. You can reverse an allocation to undo the match. See
[Apply credit](/sales/apply-credit).

### Settlement kind [#settlement-kind]

How a receipt or payment relates to documents: **Advance** (no invoice or bill yet),
**Against open items** (pays named invoices or bills), **Money back from supplier**
(a supplier pays back unapplied debit notes or payment advances through a receipt), or **Direct**
(money for a sale or expense recorded without an invoice or bill).

### Counter sale [#counter-sale]

A sale paid on the spot. You create the invoice and record the money in one step.

## Documents and their life

### Document [#document]

Any record that changes your books: an invoice, receipt, bill, payment, credit note,
debit note, journal or opening balance.

### Draft [#draft]

A saved document that has not changed your books yet. You can edit or discard it.
Invoices and bills can be drafts.

### Post [#post]

To make a document final. Posting writes its journal entry and gives it its number.
A posted document cannot be edited.

### Cancel and reversal [#reversal]

You correct a posted document by cancelling it. Accly Books then writes a **reversal**:
the same entry with debits and credits swapped, so the effect becomes zero. Both stay
on record.

### Amend [#amend]

Correcting a posted invoice or bill: Accly Books cancels it and opens a new draft with
the same details for you to fix and post.

### Purchase order (PO) [#purchase-order]

The order a customer sends you, or you send a supplier, before goods move. Accly Books
does not record purchase orders; quote the PO number as the reference.

### Invoice [#invoice]

The document you issue to a customer for goods or services sold. See
[Invoices](/sales/invoices).

### Tax invoice and bill of supply [#tax-invoice]

Under GST, an invoice that charges GST is a **tax invoice**. An invoice for exempt
supplies, or from a composition dealer, is a **bill of supply** and charges no GST.

### MRP [#mrp]

Maximum retail price: the highest price printed on packaged goods. An item can carry its
MRP for reference.

### Trade discount [#trade-discount]

A price reduction shown on the invoice, before GST. GST applies to the price after the
discount.

### Taxable value [#taxable-value]

The amount GST is charged on: the price after discount, before GST.

### Receipt [#receipt]

The document for money you receive, by cash, UPI, cheque or bank transfer. See
[Receipts](/sales/receipts).

### Bill [#bill]

The invoice a supplier sends you, recorded in your books. See [Bills](/purchases/bills).

### Payment [#payment]

The document for money you pay out. See [Payments](/purchases/payments).

### Credit note [#credit-note]

A document that reduces what a customer owes, for a sales return or a price reduction.
It gives the customer a credit that you apply to an invoice or refund. See
[Credit notes](/sales/credit-notes).

### Debit note [#debit-note]

A document that reduces what you owe a supplier, for a purchase return, short supply or
price reduction. Apply its credit to a bill or record a supplier refund with a receipt.
See [Debit notes](/purchases/debit-notes).

### Opening balance and cutover [#opening-balance]

The **cutover** is the day you move your books into Accly Books. The **opening balance**
records every account's balance at that date, so your books start where the old ones
ended. See [Opening balance](/setup/opening-balance).

### Opening item (opening claim and opening credit) [#opening-item]

An unpaid invoice, bill or unused advance carried over at the cutover. An **opening
claim** is an amount still owed (an old unpaid invoice or bill); an **opening credit**
is an old advance or credit. You settle them like normal documents.

## GST

### GST [#gst]

Goods and Services Tax, charged on most sales in India at rates such as 5% or 18%.

### GSTIN [#gstin]

A business's 15-character GST registration number, for example `27AAPFS1234K1ZW`. The
first two digits are the state code; characters 3–12 are the PAN.

### CGST, SGST and IGST [#cgst-sgst-igst]

Within one state, GST splits equally into central tax (**CGST**) and state tax
(**SGST**): 18% becomes 9% + 9%. Between states, the full rate is integrated tax
(**IGST**).

### Place of supply [#place-of-supply]

The state where GST law treats the sale as made. If it matches your state, you charge
CGST and SGST; otherwise IGST.

### HSN and SAC [#hsn-sac]

Codes that classify goods (**HSN**, for example 8481 for valves) and services (**SAC**)
on invoices and returns.

### Taxable, exempt and nil-rated [#supply-class]

The **supply class** of an account or item. **Taxable** supplies carry GST. **Exempt**
supplies carry none by law, such as most school fees. **Nil-rated** supplies carry a 0%
rate.

### Input tax credit (ITC) [#itc]

GST you paid on purchases that you can deduct from the GST you collect on sales. A bill
line marked ITC-eligible books its GST as an input credit instead of a cost.

### Output tax [#output-tax]

GST you charge customers on sales. You pay it to the government after deducting ITC.

### GST register [#gst-register]

The list of GST charged on sales (outward) or paid on purchases (inward) in a period,
used to prepare returns.

### B2B, B2CL and B2CS [#b2b-b2c]

GSTR-1 sections for sales: **B2B** to registered businesses (with a GSTIN), **B2CL**
large inter-state sales to consumers, **B2CS** other sales to consumers.

### State code [#state-code]

The two-digit GST code of an Indian state, such as 27 for Maharashtra and 29 for
Karnataka. It starts every GSTIN.

### Reverse charge [#reverse-charge]

A rule under which the buyer, not the supplier, pays the GST on certain purchases.
Accly Books does not compute reverse charge yet; ask your CA how to record it.

### E-way bill and lorry receipt (LR) [#e-way-bill]

An **e-way bill** is the electronic permit to move goods worth more than ₹50,000. A
**lorry receipt** is the transporter's receipt for goods it carries. Accly Books does
not create either; quote their numbers in the narration.

### Set-off [#set-off]

Using input tax credit to pay output tax in the GST return, so you pay only the
difference in cash.

### Cess [#cess]

An extra tax on some goods, such as tobacco or cars, charged on top of GST.

### GSTR-1, GSTR-2B and GSTR-3B [#gstr]

Monthly or quarterly GST returns. **GSTR-1** reports your outward supplies (sales):
invoice by invoice for registered buyers and large inter-state sales, and in summary
for other consumer sales. **GSTR-2B** is the monthly statement of purchase invoices your suppliers reported,
which shows the input tax credit you can claim. **GSTR-3B** is the summary return on
which you pay the tax.

## TDS and identity

### TDS [#tds]

Tax deducted at source: when you make a payment the law lists, such as a professional
fee, rent, a contractor's bill or a large purchase of goods, you keep back a percentage
as income tax and deposit it with the government on the payee's behalf. Example: on
a ₹50,000 professional fee at 10%, you pay ₹45,000 and deposit ₹5,000.

### TDS section [#tds-section]

The legal category that sets the TDS rate. From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act 2025
uses new codes, such as **1027** for professional services (formerly section 194J) at
10%.

### TDS Payable [#tds-payable]

The account holding TDS you deducted but have not yet deposited.

### TDS register [#tds-register]

The list of TDS deducted in a period, by section and party, used for TDS returns.

### Challan [#challan]

The government form for paying tax. TDS is deposited with challan ITNS 281.

### Form 26AS [#form-26as]

The tax statement where a business sees TDS that others deducted from payments to it.
Customers' TDS on your receipts should appear there.

### PAN [#pan]

Permanent Account Number: a 10-character income-tax ID, for example `AAPFS1234K`.

## Money movement

### Money account [#money-account]

A cash box or bank account: an account that holds money. Receipts and payments move
money in and out of one.

### Payment method [#payment-method]

How money moves, such as **Cash**, **HDFC UPI** or **ICICI NEFT**. Each method is tied
to one cash or bank account. See [Banking](/setup/banking).

### UPI, NEFT, RTGS and UTR [#upi-neft]

**UPI** is instant mobile payment. **NEFT** and **RTGS** are bank transfers. A **UTR**
is the unique reference number of a bank transfer; record it as the reference.

## Control and periods

### Lock [#lock]

A date up to which no one can post, cancel or change entries. The **books lock** covers
all entries; the **tax lock** covers entries that affect GST or TDS, usually set after
a return is filed. See [Locks](/accounting/locks).

### Books lock and tax period lock [#books-lock]

The two locks in **Settings → Locks**. The **books lock** stops every change on or before
its date. The **tax period lock** stops changes that affect GST or TDS on or before its
date.

### Lock exception [#lock-exception]

Time-limited permission for one member to post on or before the locked date, for a late
correction. It expires on its own.

### Roles [#roles]

What a member may do in an organization: **Owner**, **Accountant**, **Chartered
Accountant** (read and review) or **Operator** (day-to-day entry). See
[Roles and access](/getting-started/roles-and-access).

### Audit log [#audit-log]

The record of sensitive actions: who did what and when. See
[Audit log](/administration/audit-log).
